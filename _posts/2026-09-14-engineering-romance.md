## Numbers as a private joke

In 2004, Google's IPO filing with the SEC didn't ask for a round number. It asked to raise $2,718,281,828, precisely $1 billion times e, the base of the natural logarithm, correct to nine decimal places. It confused enough journalists at the time that papers had to explain the joke. Twelve years later, Google did it again: a 2016 stock buyback for $7,019,340,976.83, which is e raised to the 26th power. Twenty-six is the number of letters in "Alphabet," the parent company renamed two years earlier.

Nvidia's acquisition of Hugging Face this year carries the same fingerprint. The price: $12,930,300,000. Strip the punctuation and 129303 is the decimal value of U+1F917, the Unicode code point for 🤗, officially named "Hugging Face." People kept looking and found more folded into the same number: 93 for Nvidia's 1993 founding, 12 for its $12 IPO price, 03 for its three founders, Jensen Huang, Chris Malachowsky, and Curtis Priem. Hugging Face's CEO Clément Delangue confirmed the number was deliberate, adding that he was surprised nobody noticed sooner: "price was never really the most important thing for both sides."

## Names as devotion

Apple named its first computer with a graphical interface Lisa, after Steve Jobs's daughter, born the same year it shipped. Josh Wardle built a word-guessing game for his partner Palak Shah because she loved that kind of puzzle, then named it after his own surname: Wordle.

The most striking version of this sits in Chinese. Baidu traces its own name to a Song-dynasty poem, Xin Qiji's Green Jade Cup: Lantern Festival Night, written roughly 900 years ago. Its famous closing lines: "I searched for her a thousand times in the crowd; suddenly, turning back, there she was, in the fading lantern light." Robin Li picked the name as a description of what a search engine actually does, looking, relentlessly, for the thing you're trying to find.

Pony Ma tells a similar story about Tencent's own QQ, the company's first product. He met his future wife chatting on QQ, and when she finally asked who she was talking to, he answered the way the app itself would: "Penguin Dad," after QQ's mascot. She wrote back "Penguin Mum." They married six months after meeting in person.

## Names as an answer to doubt

SpaceX named its capsule Dragon after "Puff the Magic Dragon," the 1963 folk song, because early critics treated the whole idea of a reusable capsule as a fantasy. An engineer who worked there at the time has said Musk was humming the tune after a rough meeting with Congress, not long before the name stuck.

Tesla wanted Model S, Model E, and Model X, spelling something the marketing team clearly had in mind. Ford's trademark on "Model E" blocked it, so Tesla shipped Model 3 instead, its logo for a while a backward E rendered as three stacked bars. Years later Ford launched its own EV line under the name Tesla couldn't use: Model e.

## Jokes that got promoted to real

HTTP status code 418, "I'm a teapot," started as an April Fools' joke in a 1998 RFC satirizing how bloated the HTTP spec was getting. In 2017, an IETF chair tried to have it quietly removed from mainstream implementations. Developers pushed back hard enough that it stayed, and the current HTTP specification, RFC 9110, now formally reserves 418 so no real status code can ever claim it. .NET, Rust, Rails, Go, Symfony, and Python all still implement it. Google's own servers still answer at google.com/teapot with an animated pot.

Chip designers did something similar with silicon itself. Metal layers on a die are invisible to the naked eye, readable only under 200 to 500 times magnification, which made them a quiet canvas through the 1970s and 80s. Intel hid designer Federico Faggin's initials on the 4004, the company's first microprocessor, in 1971. A public archive at Florida State University, the Silicon Zoo, catalogs more than eighty of these: cartoon characters, logos, one Qualcomm engineer's drawing of a pickup truck, done for his four-year-old son.

NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory has been doing the same thing on other planets. Curiosity's wheels are punched with a pattern that spells JPL in Morse code, stamped into the Martian soil on every drive, useful for tracking the rover's visual odometry and also just there because engineer Matt Maimone proposed it as a signature. Nine years later, Perseverance's parachute unfurled over Mars with a hidden message woven into its red-and-white pattern: DARE MIGHTY THINGS, a line from Theodore Roosevelt, plus JPL's own GPS coordinates in the outer ring. Systems engineer Ian Clark, a crossword hobbyist, built it in. Only about six people knew before the mission's landing lead dared the internet to solve it live during the broadcast. It took a few hours. Clark's only complaint afterward: "Next time, I'll have to be a little bit more creative."

## A love letter that left the solar system

In 1977, two Voyager probes launched carrying a golden record each, meant to explain Earth to whoever might eventually find it: music, greetings in 55 languages, the sound of a mother's heartbeat. Creative director Ann Druyan arranged to have her own brainwaves recorded for it, an hour of her thoughts and feelings translated into sound. She was falling in love at the time with the project's lead scientist, Carl Sagan. They married two years later and stayed married until his death in 1996. Their courtship is now on a spacecraft that has left the solar system.

Writer Timothy Ferris, who also worked on the record, added something nobody was likely to ever find: a private dedication hand-etched into the blank runout groove at the very end, a part of the record with no audio track, nothing to play. It reads: "To the makers of music — all worlds, all times."

## What it adds up to

None of this changes how any of it works. A status code still returns 418 for the same reason it returns 404. A parachute still slows a landing whether or not anyone ever reads the pattern woven into it. An acquisition price still closes the same deal whether or not anyone finds the joke buried in it. But somewhere between the spec and the filing and the merge button, someone decided the number or the name was also theirs to say something with, and said it anyway, on the record, where it would sit for however long the system keeps running.

## 數字裡的私人玩笑

2004年，Google 的 IPO 文件向 SEC 申報的集資金額，不是一個整數。文件寫的是2,718,281,828美元，剛好是10億乘以 e，自然對數的底數，準到小數點後九位。當年不少記者要先查字典，才看得懂這個玩笑。十二年後，Google 又來一次：2016年一次股份回購，金額7,019,340,976.83美元，是 e 的26次方。26正正是「Alphabet」這個字的字母數目，兩年前才改名做母公司的名字。

今年 Nvidia 收購 Hugging Face，同一種手法再現。收購價12,930,300,000美元。拆走標點，129303正正是 Unicode 編碼 U+1F917的十進制數值，也就是🤗這個 emoji，官方名稱正是「Hugging Face」。有人繼續留意，還在同一個數字裡找到更多：93對應 Nvidia 1993年成立，12對應上市價12美元，03對應三位創辦人：Jensen Huang、Chris Malachowsky、Curtis Priem。Hugging Face 行政總裁 Clément Delangue 事後確認這是刻意安排，還說自己驚訝沒有人早點發現：「價錢由頭到尾都不是雙方最在意的事。」

## 命名裡的心意

Apple 第一部有圖形介面的電腦叫 Lisa，用史提夫喬布斯女兒的名字命名，出生年份跟電腦上市同一年。工程師 Josh Wardle 為拍拖對象 Palak Shah 做了一個猜字遊戲，因為她喜歡這類文字謎題，之後用自己的姓氏諧音命名：Wordle。

這類故事裡最動人的一個在中文世界。百度自己講述的名字由來，追溯到大約九百年前，辛棄疾一首宋詞《青玉案·元夕》。詞的結尾兩句最為人熟悉：「眾裡尋他千百度，驀然回首，那人卻在，燈火闌珊處。」寫的是在擁擠的元宵燈會裡，反覆尋找一個人，直到不經意回頭，才發現對方就在燈火漸暗的地方。李彥宏用這句話形容搜尋引擎實際在做的事，鍥而不捨，去找一樣正在尋找的東西。

馬化騰講過一個關於騰訊 QQ 的類似故事，QQ 是騰訊的第一個產品。他在 QQ 聊天認識了未來的太太，對方問他是誰，他就用 QQ 那隻吉祥物回答：「企鵝爸爸」。對方回了句「企鵝媽媽」。兩人見面之後半年結婚。

## 命名裡的不忿氣

SpaceX 將太空艙叫做 Dragon，出自1963年民歌《Puff the Magic Dragon》，因為早期評論界普遍將整個可重複使用太空艙的構想當成天方夜譚。一位當年在場的工程師憶述，馬斯克開完一場與國會的艱難會議之後，一直哼著這首歌的旋律，不久之後這個名字就定了下來。

Tesla 原本想要 Model S、Model E、Model X，拼出來的意思，行銷團隊心裡有數。福特擁有「Model E」的商標，攔住了這個計劃，Tesla 於是改推 Model 3，logo 一度用三條疊起來的橫槓，砌成一個反轉的 E。幾年之後，福特自己推出電動車系列，用的正是當年不准 Tesla 用的名字：Model e。

## 升格做真事的玩笑

HTTP 狀態碼418「我是茶煲」，源自1998年一份 April Fools' RFC，諷刺當時 HTTP 規格越寫越臃腫。2017年，一位 IETF 主席曾經想悄悄將它從主流實現裡拿走。開發者強烈反彈，令它留了下來，現行 HTTP 規格 RFC 9110更正式將418永久保留，任何真正的狀態碼都不可以再用這個編號。.NET、Rust、Rails、Go、Symfony 與 Python 至今仍然實現這個狀態碼。Google 自己的伺服器，在 google.com/teapot 依然會回應一個動畫茶煲。

晶片設計師在矽晶片本身做過類似的事。晶粒的金屬層肉眼看不見，要放大200至500倍才看得到，七、八十年代因此成為一塊安靜的畫布。Intel 在1971年推出第一款微處理器4004時，就在晶片裡藏了設計師 Federico Faggin 的縮寫「F.F.」。佛羅里達州立大學一個叫 Silicon Zoo 的公開存檔，收錄了八十幾個類似例子：卡通角色、公司標誌，還有一位 Qualcomm 工程師畫的一架小貨車，靈感來自他四歲的兒子。

NASA 噴射推進實驗室（JPL）在其他星球上做過同一件事。Curiosity 的車輪上打了一圈孔洞，用摩斯密碼拼出「JPL」，每次行駛都印在火星地面上，一方面用來輔助視覺定位，另一方面純粹是工程師 Matt Maimone 提議加上去的簽名。九年後，Perseverance 的降落傘在火星上空展開，圖案裡藏了一句話：DARE MIGHTY THINGS，出自老羅斯福總統一句演講，外圈還藏了 JPL 自己的 GPS 座標。負責系統工程的 Ian Clark 平時喜歡砌填字遊戲，親手把訊息編了進去。降落前只有大約六個人知情，任務降落總指揮在直播裡公開邀請全網解謎，幾個鐘頭就有人破解。Clark 事後唯一的感想：「下次要再花多點心思了。」

## 一封離開太陽系的情書

1977年，兩艘 Voyager 探測器升空，各自帶著一張金色唱片，準備向任何有機會發現它的生命解釋地球是什麼：音樂、55種語言的問候、一位母親心跳的錄音。負責創意總監的 Ann Druyan，當時特意將自己的腦電波錄了進去，一小時的思緒與感受，轉成聲音收錄在唱片裡。她那時正在同計劃首席科學家 Carl Sagan 拍拖，兩人兩年後結婚，一直維持到1996年 Sagan 過身為止。他們的戀情，如今在一艘早已離開太陽系的太空船上。

同樣參與這個項目的作家 Timothy Ferris，另外加了一樣幾乎不會有人找到的東西：一句私人題詞，親手刻在唱片邊緣一圈沒有聲軌的空白溝紋裡，那個位置本來什麼都播不出來。題詞寫著：「獻給音樂的創作者——所有世界，所有時代。」

## 加起來是什麼

以上一切都不會改變任何一套系統實際怎樣運作。狀態碼418照樣因為同一個原因返回，與404沒有分別。一副降落傘照樣減慢降落速度，不管有沒有人讀懂織進圖案裡的訊息。一宗收購案，不管有沒有人發現藏在價錢裡的玩笑，都照樣完成交割。只不過在規格書與申報文件與一個 merge 掣之間某個位置，總有人覺得那個數字或者那個名字，也輪到自己講一句話，然後真的講了出來，寫進紀錄裡，留在系統還在運行的每一天。
